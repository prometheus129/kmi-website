## Morocco: Gateway Market with FTA Complexity

Morocco imports an estimated 140,000 to 175,000 metric tons of polymer resins annually — the smallest of the three North African markets but the fastest growing. The country is 100% import-dependent for polymer resins, with no domestic polymerization capacity.

China has the highest market penetration of any origin, supplying an estimated 35-40% of Morocco's polymer imports. This is a striking achievement given Morocco's trade agreement landscape: the EU, United States, and Turkey all enjoy 0% duty access to Morocco under bilateral free trade agreements. China has no FTA with Morocco. Every kilogram of Chinese polymer entering Morocco pays full MFN duty.

Chinese origin succeeds despite this tariff disadvantage because of structural economics. CTO and PDH feedstock routes deliver FOB prices low enough to absorb 2.5-10% MFN duties and still compete landed against European, American, and Turkish alternatives that enter duty-free. The math works especially well for PP, PVC, and commodity PE grades where Chinese producers hold the largest cost advantages.

For a detailed analysis of how these production routes affect pricing, see our [CTO/PDH feedstock advantage explainer for MENA](/insights/cto-pdh-feedstock-advantage-mena).

Three additional factors drive China's market position in Morocco:

**Grade breadth.** Morocco's conversion industry spans packaging, automotive components, agriculture (greenhouse film, irrigation), and construction. This requires a wide grade portfolio — from LLDPE film grades to PP injection molding grades to PVC pipe compounds. China's 1,600+ producers and 600+ merchants provide broader grade availability than any single FTA-origin country. Our [Chinese producer guide for Arab importers](/insights/chinese-producer-guide-arab-importers) maps the key producers by product specialization.

**Automotive-driven engineering polymer demand.** Morocco has become a major automotive manufacturing hub, with Renault and Stellantis operating assembly plants. Automotive polymer demand includes PP (bumpers, interiors, under-hood components), PA (engine parts), ABS (dashboard panels), and other engineering resins. These specialty grades are often unavailable from Middle Eastern or Turkish producers and must be sourced from Asian or European origins.

**Business environment.** Morocco has the most accessible business environment of the three North African markets — simpler customs processes than Algeria, more stable currency than Egypt, and a well-developed network of freight forwarders and customs brokers at Casablanca and Tanger Med.

## HS Codes and Duty Structure

Morocco's tariff regime is uniquely complex because multiple FTAs create different duty rates depending on origin. Chinese importers must understand not just their own MFN rate but the rate their competitors pay.

| Product | HS Code | MFN (China) | EU Rate | US Rate | Turkey Rate | GAFTA (Saudi/UAE) |
|---------|---------|-------------|---------|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| LDPE / LLDPE granules | 3901.10.xx | **2.5%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** |
| HDPE granules | 3901.20.xx | **2.5%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** |
| PP homopolymer | 3902.10.xx | **2.5%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** |
| PP copolymers | 3902.30.xx | **2.5-10%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** |
| PVC (unplasticized) | 3904.10.xx | **10%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** |
| Engineering polymers | Various | **Varies** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** | **0%** |

Key observations:

**China faces the widest competitive tariff gap in North Africa.** In Egypt, China competes mainly against GAFTA origins. In Algeria, against GAFTA and EU. In Morocco, China competes against EU, US, Turkey, AND GAFTA — all at 0%. Morocco is the market where China's structural cost advantage must work the hardest.

**PE and PP primary forms carry the lowest MFN rate.** At 2.5% MFN duty on PE and PP granules, the tariff gap is narrow enough that Chinese CTO/PDH cost advantages easily overcome it. This is why China holds 35-40% market share despite having no FTA.

**PVC now carries a higher MFN rate than PE and PP.** Morocco's 2026 Finance Law (PLF 2026) raised the duty on primary-form PVC from 2.5% to 10% to deter redirected Chinese imports — so PVC now sits well above the 2.5% on PE and PP, though still far below finished-article rates. At 10%, Chinese origin remains competitive because the calcium carbide production route delivers a large FOB advantage. Engineering-polymer rates vary by subheading — verify the exact rate before sourcing.

**VAT at 20% applies equally to all origins.** Morocco's VAT is the highest of the three North African markets. It is calculated on CIF value plus duty, so it amplifies the absolute cost of tariff differentials but does not change the relative competitive position between origins.

**Morocco-China FTA is not on the horizon.** Unlike some markets where FTA negotiations are in progress, there is no active Morocco-China FTA negotiation. Chinese exporters should plan for MFN rates to persist.

## Tanger Med: Africa's Largest Port

Morocco's port infrastructure has been transformed by the development of Tanger Med, now the largest port in Africa by container throughput capacity.

### Transit Times from China

| Origin Port | Destination | Transit (Days) | Route | Notes |
|------------|-------------|----------------|-------|-------|
| Shanghai | Tanger Med | 20-28 | Via Suez, Mediterranean | Modern hub, fast turnaround |
| Ningbo | Tanger Med | 20-28 | Via Suez, Mediterranean | Comparable schedule |
| Shanghai | Casablanca | 22-30 | Via Suez, Mediterranean/Atlantic | Traditional polymer import port |
| Shanghai | Agadir | 24-32 | Via Suez, Atlantic | Serves southern Morocco |

**Tanger Med** is a modern deep-water transshipment hub located at the Strait of Gibraltar. Its advantages for polymer importers include: fast vessel turnaround times, direct connectivity to major Asian shipping lines, bonded warehouse and free zone facilities, and excellent road and rail connections to the Tangier-Kenitra economic corridor.

**Casablanca** remains Morocco's traditional commercial port and handles the majority of polymer imports. It is optimal for importers serving the Casablanca-Settat industrial corridor — Morocco's largest concentration of plastic conversion capacity. Port infrastructure is older than Tanger Med but customs brokerage services for polymer imports are deeply established.

**Strategic choice:** Many importers are gradually shifting volume to Tanger Med for better handling efficiency, while maintaining Casablanca relationships for its proximity to end-use markets. Both ports offer adequate service for polymer cargo.

**Customs clearance** in Morocco is generally faster than Algeria and comparable to Egypt. Allow 3-7 business days for standard clearance with complete documentation. Morocco has invested in customs modernization, including electronic filing systems, which has reduced average clearance times.

## Landed Cost: China vs EU vs Saudi Arabia

The critical question for Moroccan polymer buyers: when does China's FOB advantage overcome the tariff gap against duty-free origins?

### PP Homopolymer — Three-Way Comparison

| Cost Component | China Origin | EU Origin (Spain) | Saudi Origin |
|---------------|-------------|-------------------|-------------|
| CFR Morocco (market assessment) | $910/MT | $990/MT | $960/MT |
| Customs Duty | 2.5% = $23 | 0% (FTA) = $0 | 0% (GAFTA) = $0 |
| Subtotal (CIF + Duty) | $933 | $990 | $960 |
| VAT (20%) | $187 | $198 | $192 |
| Port handling | ~$20-30/MT | ~$20-30/MT | ~$20-30/MT |
| **Approximate Landed Cost** | **$1,140-1,150/MT** | **$1,208-1,218/MT** | **$1,172-1,182/MT** |

**China wins on PP** despite paying 2.5% duty that EU and Saudi origins avoid. The $60-80/MT FOB advantage from CTO/PDH production routes more than compensates. This explains China's dominant market share.

### HDPE Film Grade — Three-Way Comparison

| Cost Component | China Origin | EU Origin | Saudi Origin |
|---------------|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| CFR Morocco (market assessment) | $980/MT | $1,040/MT | $1,000/MT |
| Customs Duty | 2.5% = $25 | 0% = $0 | 0% = $0 |
| Subtotal (CIF + Duty) | $1,005 | $1,040 | $1,000 |
| VAT (20%) | $201 | $208 | $200 |
| Port handling | ~$20-30/MT | ~$20-30/MT | ~$20-30/MT |
| **Approximate Landed Cost** | **$1,226-1,236/MT** | **$1,268-1,278/MT** | **$1,220-1,230/MT** |

**HDPE is tighter.** Saudi origin (ethane-based, 0% GAFTA duty) and Chinese origin land within $5-10/MT of each other. In PE, the cost advantage of Saudi ethane-based production partially offsets China's CTO/PDH advantage, and the 2.5% duty tips the balance. Origin selection in PE depends on current market conditions, grade availability, and payment terms.

### PVC — Where Tariff Hurts Most

| Cost Component | China Origin | EU Origin |
|---------------|-------------|-----------|
| CFR Morocco (market assessment) | $720/MT | $810/MT |
| Customs Duty | 10% = $72 | 0% (FTA) = $0 |
| Subtotal (CIF + Duty) | $792 | $810 |

At the current 10% MFN duty — raised from 2.5% under PLF 2026 — Chinese origin remains competitive because the calcium carbide cost advantage is large: the $792/MT subtotal still undercuts EU origin's $810/MT before port charges. Verify the current applicable rate for your specific HS subheading before sourcing, as Morocco has been adjusting PVC duties.

## Automotive Sector Polymer Demand

Morocco's emergence as a major automotive manufacturing hub is reshaping polymer import demand in ways that favor Chinese engineering polymer sourcing.

**Renault-Nissan** operates a large assembly plant in Tangier, producing vehicles primarily for European and African markets. **Stellantis** operates a plant in Kenitra. Together, these facilities and their tier-1/tier-2 supplier ecosystem have made Morocco the largest passenger vehicle producer in Africa.

**Polymer demand from automotive manufacturing includes:**

- **PP compounds** — bumper fascias, interior trim, instrument panel substrates, door panels, pillar covers. Automotive PP grades require specific MFI ranges, impact modification, and color matching that commodity PP does not satisfy.
- **ABS** — dashboard components, center console parts, exterior trim elements. ABS for automotive requires consistent flow properties and surface quality.
- **PA6 and PA66** — engine covers, intake manifolds, connectors, fuel system components. Nylon grades must meet thermal, chemical, and mechanical specifications per OEM standards.
- **PBT and POM** — electrical connectors, gear mechanisms, fastening components. These engineering polymers require tight dimensional tolerances.
- **PP/EPDM and TPO compounds** — exterior body panels, bumper covers, fender liners.

**Where Chinese origin fits in automotive.** Most automotive OEM specifications reference European or Japanese polymer producers (BASF, DuPont, Syensqo (formerly Solvay), Mitsubishi). However, Chinese engineering polymer producers — particularly Wanhua (MDI/polyurethane and engineering polymers), Kingfa (PA, PBT compounds), and several smaller specialists — increasingly offer automotive-qualified grades at materially lower prices. The transition from specification-locked European grades to qualified Chinese alternatives represents a growing procurement opportunity for Moroccan automotive tier suppliers.

## Payment and MAD Stability

Morocco offers the most stable financial environment of the three North African markets, with straightforward payment processes and predictable currency behavior.

**The Moroccan Dirham (MAD)** is pegged to a basket weighted approximately 60% Euro and 40% US Dollar, managed by Bank Al-Maghrib (the central bank). This basket peg has delivered low currency volatility compared to Egypt's pound or Algeria's dinar. For polymer importers buying in USD, MAD/USD exchange rate movements are modest and largely predictable within a band.

**Payment terms.** Moroccan importers typically use irrevocable L/C at sight for initial transactions with Chinese suppliers, transitioning to CAD (cash against documents) or T/T terms as relationships develop. Morocco does not impose the strict bank domiciliation requirements that Algeria does — the import payment process is closer to international norms.

**No significant forex restrictions.** Morocco's foreign exchange regime is convertible for current account transactions (including import payments). Importers do not face the forex rationing or allocation constraints common in Algeria. Payment execution timelines are predictable — typically 3-5 business days for L/C processing through Moroccan commercial banks.

**Bank infrastructure.** Morocco's commercial banking sector (Attijariwafa Bank, BMCE Bank of Africa, Banque Populaire) is well-developed and experienced in trade finance for Asian origins. Documentary credit processing is efficient by regional standards.

## Common Import Errors

**1. Ignoring the FTA landscape.** Moroccan buyers sometimes compare Chinese origin only against Saudi prices without considering EU origin at 0% duty. For specialty grades and engineering polymers, European producers entering at 0% duty may be more competitive than expected. Always run a three-way (or four-way, including Turkey) landed cost comparison.

**2. Misclassifying PVC subheadings.** Primary-form PVC resin attracts 10% MFN duty (raised from 2.5% under PLF 2026); PVC compounds, PVC paste, and finished PVC articles attract different — generally higher — rates. Verify the exact subheading with Moroccan customs (Administration des Douanes et Impots Indirects) before quoting to end customers.

**3. Overlooking Tanger Med advantages.** Importers with historical relationships at Casablanca sometimes do not evaluate whether Tanger Med offers better transit times, handling efficiency, or free zone benefits for their specific operations. At minimum, obtain comparative quotes from brokers at both ports.

**4. Underestimating automotive specification requirements.** Importers serving automotive tier suppliers must ensure Chinese-origin polymers meet OEM material specifications, not just generic grade equivalencies. Automotive applications require specific test certifications, material data sheets conforming to IMDS (International Material Data System), and sometimes plant-specific audits. Verify compliance before committing to supply contracts.
