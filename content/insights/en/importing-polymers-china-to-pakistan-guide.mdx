## Why China-Origin Polymers for Pakistani Buyers

Pakistan imports an estimated 1.5 to 2 million metric tons of polymers annually — polyethylene, polypropylene, and PVC — with zero domestic PE or PP production capacity. Engro Polymer and Chemicals Limited operates the country's only PVC resin plant at approximately 295,000 MT per year (following the PVC III expansion which added 100,000 MT/yr capacity, operational since early 2022), leaving the entire PE and PP market dependent on imports.

The Middle East is Pakistan's dominant polymer origin, supplying an estimated 56% of import volumes. SABIC, Borouge, and ORPIC have built deep relationships with Pakistani distributors over decades, supported by a decisive transit time advantage: 3-7 days from Gulf ports to Karachi versus 18-25 days from Chinese origins.

Despite this, China's share of Pakistan's polymer imports is growing — currently estimated at approximately 11%. The driver is not a tariff preference, as is often assumed: it is China's delivered price on feedstock-advantaged grades (notably PP), the breadth of its grade catalog, and its ability to supply PVC volumes beyond Engro's domestic output.

A widespread misconception holds that the China-Pakistan Free Trade Agreement (CPFTA) Phase II — in force since January 1, 2020 — gives Chinese commodity polymers a preferential duty rate. It does not. Commodity PE, PP, and PVC sit on Pakistan's CPFTA "no-concession" (sensitive) list, so they are excluded from the agreement's tariff cuts: Chinese-origin commodity polymers pay Pakistan's full MFN customs duty, exactly as Middle Eastern, Korean, and other origins do. There is no CPFTA duty edge on these grades — a point we detail in the duty section below.

Three additional factors support China as a complementary sourcing origin:

**Feedstock cost advantage.** Chinese producers operating on coal-to-olefin (CTO) and propane dehydrogenation (PDH) routes produce PE and PP at structurally lower variable cost than naphtha-dependent producers when crude oil prices are elevated. Note: for PE specifically, Middle Eastern ethane-based production costs are comparable to or lower than CTO — China's feedstock advantage is most pronounced on PP. For a detailed analysis, see our [CTO/PDH feedstock advantage explainer](/insights/cto-pdh-naphtha-feedstock-advantage-resin-prices).

**Supply breadth.** China's polymer export market involves over 1,600 producers and more than 600 active trading merchants. This depth provides grade selection and competitive pricing that no single Middle Eastern producer can match across all applications. Our [Chinese producer landscape guide](/insights/china-polymer-producers-guide) maps the key producers by feedstock route and product specialization.

**Grade availability.** Engineering polymers, specialty compounds, and PVC grades beyond Engro's domestic output are readily available from Chinese origins. For Pakistani converters requiring specific MFI ranges, copolymer formulations, or PVC K-values, China offers the broadest catalog.

The honest assessment: Middle Eastern origins will remain dominant for commodity PE and PP in Pakistan due to the overwhelming transit time advantage and comparable (or lower) ethane-based feedstock costs. Because there is no tariff difference between the origins, China's opportunity rests on three things — delivered price on feedstock-advantaged grades (notably PP via CTO/PDH when crude is elevated), the breadth of its grade catalog for specialty and engineering polymers where ME availability is limited, and PVC, where domestic Engro capacity is insufficient and import volumes are needed regardless of origin. For a detailed origin comparison, see our [Pakistan origin comparison analysis](/insights/pakistan-china-polymer-origin-comparison).

## HS Codes, Duties, and the Tax Stack

Pakistan's import tariff regime is more complex than a single duty rate. Pakistani importers face a cascading stack of taxes and levies on top of the base customs duty. Understanding each layer — and which ones China-origin polymers actually pay — is essential for accurate cost modeling.

### Customs Duty: MFN Applies to China and the Middle East Alike

| Product | HS Code | CPFTA Concession? | Applied Duty (MFN, all origins) |
|---------|---------|-------------------|----------------------------------|
| LDPE / LLDPE | 3901.10 | No (sensitive list) | ~5% (5-11% band — verify) |
| HDPE | 3901.20 | No (sensitive list) | ~5% (5-11% band — verify) |
| PP homopolymer | 3902.10 | No (sensitive list) | ~5% (5-11% band — verify) |
| PP copolymers | 3902.30 | No (sensitive list) | ~5% (5-11% band — verify) |
| PVC resin | 3904.10 / .21 / .22 | No (sensitive list) | ~15-20% (verify) |

**The CPFTA does not cut duty on commodity polymers.** PE, PP, and PVC are on Pakistan's CPFTA sensitive / "no-concession" track, so the agreement provides no preferential rate on these grades — Chinese-origin resin pays the full MFN customs duty. Middle Eastern origins have no FTA with Pakistan either, so they pay the same MFN. The tariff treatment is therefore origin-neutral: it is not a reason to prefer China, nor a reason to prefer the Middle East. Exact subheading rates should be verified against the FBR First Schedule (Pakistan Customs Tariff, FY2025-26) or via WeBOC, as the customs-duty slabs were revised to 0/5/10/15% from July 1, 2025 (National Tariff Policy 2025-30).

### The Cascading Tax Stack

On top of the customs duty, several additional levies apply. They are origin-neutral — China and the Middle East face the same stack. Pakistani importers must account for the full picture:

| Levy | Rate | Base | Notes |
|------|------|------|-------|
| Customs Duty (CD) | ~5% PE/PP; ~15-20% PVC (MFN) | CIF value | No CPFTA concession on commodity polymers — MFN applies to all origins. Verify subheading (FBR First Schedule FY2025-26). |
| Additional Customs Duty (ACD) | 0% on PE (3901) and PP (3902) | CIF value | PE and PP exempt under SRO 1151(I)/2025; ACD is also 0% on the 0/5/10% duty slabs. PVC (3904): verify (0% if on a ≤10% slab, up to 2% otherwise). |
| Regulatory Duty (RD) | 0% typical on primary resin | CIF value | Product-dependent; confirm none applies to your subheading. |
| General Sales Tax (GST) | 18% | CIF + CD + ACD + RD | Creditable as input tax for registered manufacturers. |
| Withholding Tax (WHT) | 1-2% (manufacturer-importer); 5.5%+ (commercial importer) | CIF + CD + ACD + RD | Adjustable against income tax; not a permanent cost for filers. |

**Effective burden for a registered manufacturer-importer (PE/PP):** the customs duty (~5%) is the main permanent cost. ACD is zero (SRO 1151(I)/2025 exemption), RD is typically zero on primary resin, and GST (18%) plus WHT (1-2%) are largely recoverable or creditable. The headline cash outlay at clearance is large because GST cascades on CIF plus duties, but most of it washes back for filers. PVC carries a higher permanent duty cost (~15-20%). Verify every line against your specific subheading.

Because the customs duty is the same for all origins (no CPFTA concession), the tax stack creates no origin-based cost difference. What it does mean is that the duty you do pay is amplified: GST cascades on CIF plus duties, so each point of customs duty adds more than a point to the cash outlay at clearance. This is why getting the subheading — and therefore the correct MFN rate — right matters more than chasing a CPFTA certificate that delivers nothing on these grades.

## CPFTA Certificate of Origin: When It Helps (and When It Doesn't)

For commodity PE, PP, and PVC, a CPFTA Certificate of Origin delivers no duty benefit: these grades are on the no-concession list, so there is no preferential rate to claim, and customs applies MFN duty whether or not you present a CPFTA CO. Do not spend time or money obtaining one for these polymers expecting a tariff saving — there is none. The CPFTA CO only matters for products that actually carry a concession under the agreement. If your specific subheading is one of those, the process below applies.

**Issuing authority.** In China, the CO is issued by the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT) or by local branches of the General Administration of Customs. The exporter or their authorized agent applies for the CO before shipment.

**Rules of origin.** The product must qualify under CPFTA rules of origin, which generally require that the polymer was either wholly produced in China or substantially transformed there. For commodity polymer resins produced from Chinese-sourced or imported feedstock and polymerized in Chinese facilities, this criterion is typically met without difficulty.

**Documentation requirements.** The CO must specify: the HS code at the 6-digit level or higher, the product description matching the commercial invoice, the FOB value, the consignee in Pakistan, and the origin criteria met. Any inconsistency between the CO and other shipping documents — particularly the HS code, product description, or value — is grounds for customs rejection of the preferential rate.

**Validity.** CPFTA certificates of origin are typically valid for 12 months from the date of issuance.

**Practical advice.** First confirm your specific subheading actually carries a CPFTA concession — most commodity polymer grades do not, in which case no CO is needed for duty. If it does, request the CO from your Chinese supplier early in the order process. Verify that the HS code on the CO matches the HS code you intend to declare to Pakistan Customs. If there is any ambiguity in the HS classification, resolve it before shipment — not at the customs clearance stage where delays compound into demurrage charges.

## Port Logistics: Karachi and Port Qasim

Pakistan's polymer imports flow through two primary ports in the Karachi metropolitan area. Gwadar port, developed under the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC), is not yet a significant commercial cargo gateway for polymer imports but represents a potential future option.

### Transit Times from China

| Origin Port | Destination | Transit (Days) | Service Type |
|------------|-------------|----------------|--------------|
| Shanghai | Karachi Port | 18-25 | Direct and via transshipment (multiple weekly) |
| Ningbo | Karachi Port | 18-25 | Direct and via transshipment |
| Qingdao | Karachi Port | 20-28 | Fewer direct services; transshipment common |
| Shanghai | Port Qasim | 18-25 | Growing service frequency |
| Jebel Ali (UAE) | Karachi Port | 3-5 | Multiple daily services |
| Jubail (Saudi) | Karachi Port | 5-7 | Via transshipment at Jebel Ali or direct |

**Karachi Port (KPT)** is the older, more established facility handling the majority of Pakistan's containerized cargo. Congestion is a recurring issue, particularly during peak season. Demurrage and detention charges can accumulate rapidly during clearance delays. For polymer importers, efficient customs documentation and pre-arrival clearance preparation are essential.

**Port Qasim** is the newer deep-water port approximately 35 km east of central Karachi. It handles a growing share of container traffic and generally offers less congestion than KPT. Several polymer importers have shifted clearance operations to Port Qasim for faster turnaround.

**Gwadar Port** is being developed under CPEC with Chinese investment and is conceptually positioned as a gateway for Chinese exports to Pakistan and transit cargo to Central Asia. As of early 2026, commercial container operations remain limited and Gwadar is not a practical option for regular polymer imports. This may change as CPEC infrastructure matures.

### CPEC Context

The China-Pakistan Economic Corridor is the largest bilateral infrastructure investment in Pakistan's history. While Gwadar port and connecting road/rail infrastructure are the headline projects, CPEC's more immediate relevance for polymer importers is the broader strengthening of China-Pakistan trade facilitation, customs cooperation, and financial settlement mechanisms. These improvements are gradual but directionally supportive of increased Chinese-origin imports.

## Landed Cost Worked Example

The following illustrative example builds up the landed cost for a common Pakistani application: PP homopolymer for woven sack production. Woven PP sacks are used extensively in Pakistan's textile, agriculture, and cement sectors — with major converting clusters in Faisalabad, Karachi, and Lahore. Because commodity PP has no CPFTA concession, the tax treatment is identical for China and the Middle East — so the example shows a single build-up and isolates what actually differs between origins: the delivered CFR price.

**Assumptions:** PP homopolymer (HS 3902.10), CFR Karachi market assessment $1,050/MT, 20 MT container, registered manufacturer-importer (woven-sack converter).

### Landed Cost Build-Up (PP Homopolymer, MFN — Same for All Origins)

| Cost Component | USD/MT | Notes |
|---------------|--------|-------|
| CFR Karachi | $1,050.00 | Market assessment |
| Customs Duty (MFN ~5%) | $52.50 | No CPFTA concession; same for China and ME. Verify subheading. |
| Additional Customs Duty | $0.00 | PP (3902) ACD-exempt — SRO 1151(I)/2025 |
| Regulatory Duty | $0.00 | None typical on primary PP resin (verify) |
| **Subtotal (base for GST)** | **$1,102.50** | |
| GST (18%) | $198.45 | Creditable input tax for registered manufacturers |
| WHT (1%, manufacturer) | $11.03 | Adjustable against income tax |
| **Total cash at clearance** | **$1,311.98** | |
| **Less recoverable GST + WHT** | **−$209.48** | Credited/adjusted for filers |
| **Effective permanent cost** | **~$1,103** | ≈ CFR + ~5% duty |

**There is no CPFTA saving to capture on this grade** — a Middle Eastern shipment of the same PP at the same CFR would carry the identical duty and tax stack. The customs duty (~$52.50/MT here) is the main permanent cost; GST and WHT are recoverable for a registered manufacturer, so the effective landed cost is roughly CFR plus the ~5% duty.

Note: This example uses illustrative rates. Actual rates vary by specific HS subheading, the FBR First Schedule in force (FY2025-26), and any applicable regulatory duty. The GST component (18%) is adjustable as input tax credit for registered manufacturers and is generally recoverable; WHT is adjustable against corporate income tax. Confirm exact rates with a licensed customs broker or against WeBOC for specific shipments.

**The real decision variable is CFR, not duty.** Since both origins pay the same MFN duty and tax stack, the comparison comes down to delivered CFR Karachi price (FOB plus freight) and working-capital cost. Middle Eastern producers often quote lower CFR to Karachi because of much shorter freight (3-7 days versus 18-25). China wins when its FOB pricing is competitive enough to produce a comparable or lower CFR Karachi price despite the longer transit — most likely for CTO/PDH-advantaged grades (notably PP) during periods of elevated crude oil prices, and for grades or PVC volumes the Middle East cannot readily supply.

## Payment Terms and PKR Management

Pakistan's foreign exchange environment creates unique challenges for polymer importers that do not exist in most other Asian markets.

**Letter of Credit (L/C) is mandatory.** The State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) requires that most commercial imports be transacted via documentary Letter of Credit opened through an authorized dealer bank. This is not optional — it is a regulatory requirement. Cash-against-documents (CAD) and open account terms, which are common in Southeast Asian polymer trade, are generally not available for Pakistani importers.

**L/C opening requirements.** Pakistani banks typically require 100% margin deposit (or a percentage depending on the importer's banking relationship and credit history) to open an import L/C. This ties up significant working capital before the goods even ship. For a 20 MT container at $1,050/MT CFR, the L/C amount is approximately $21,000 — and the margin deposit may be the full amount for importers without established banking facilities.

**Dollar sourcing challenges.** Pakistan has experienced persistent dollar shortages in recent years, despite IMF stabilization programs. The PKR has depreciated significantly — trading at approximately PKR 280/USD — and the gap between official and open market exchange rates can affect the effective cost of imports. Importers must source dollars through the banking system, and delays in dollar allocation can delay L/C opening and shipment scheduling.

**PKR depreciation risk.** The 18-25 day transit from China means the PKR/USD rate at the time of customs clearance and payment may differ materially from the rate at the time of order placement. For high-volume importers, this exchange rate exposure is a meaningful cost variable. Some importers manage this by maintaining dollar-denominated accounts or by timing purchases to coincide with periods of relative PKR stability.

**Practical implications for Chinese-origin sourcing:** L/C terms are standard in China-Pakistan polymer trade, so Chinese suppliers are accustomed to this payment mechanism. The key consideration is timing — ensuring dollar allocation and L/C opening are secured before committing to shipment dates. Building a 2-3 week buffer between L/C opening and expected shipment readiness is prudent given the unpredictability of dollar allocation timelines.

## Common Import Errors

Pakistani polymer importers, particularly those expanding from Middle Eastern to Chinese origins for the first time, encounter several recurring pitfalls:

**1. Chasing a CPFTA certificate that delivers nothing.** A common error is assuming a CPFTA Certificate of Origin will cut the duty on commodity PE, PP, or PVC. It will not — these grades are on the no-concession list and pay MFN regardless of origin. Don't build your landed-cost model around a CPFTA saving that doesn't exist on these polymers; confirm whether your specific subheading carries any concession before relying on it.

**2. HS code misclassification.** Pakistan Customs uses the HS system but interpretation of subheadings can vary. A grade classified as 3901.10 (LDPE, specific gravity less than 0.94) versus 3901.20 (HDPE, specific gravity 0.94 or above) may attract different duty and regulatory duty rates. Confirm HS classification with your customs broker before shipment, not after arrival.

**3. SBP compliance failures.** Importing without a valid Form E (the SBP-required import authorization, distinct from Form M used for some non-CPFTA imports), or with an expired authorization, can result in customs holds, penalties, and difficulties in future import clearances. SBP requirements change periodically — maintain current awareness through your authorized dealer bank.

**4. Misreading the tax stack.** Two mistakes are common: assuming a CPFTA duty saving that doesn't apply to commodity polymers, and overstating the permanent burden by counting GST and WHT as costs when they are largely recoverable for a registered manufacturer. Model the customs duty (~5% PE/PP, higher for PVC) as the main permanent cost, treat ACD as zero for PE/PP (SRO 1151(I)/2025), and net out the recoverable GST/WHT. The duty and stack are the same for China and the Middle East — neither origin has a tariff edge.

**5. Dollar allocation timing.** Committing to shipment dates before securing dollar allocation through the banking system. This leads to delayed L/C opening, shipment delays, and potential price renegotiation with the Chinese supplier.

**6. Ignoring transit time working capital cost.** The 18-25 day transit from China versus 3-7 days from the Middle East means 11-22 additional days of capital tied up in transit. At prevailing Pakistani borrowing rates, this is a real cost that must be factored into the origin comparison.

**7. PSQCA standards compliance.** Pakistan Standards and Quality Control Authority may require standards compliance certification for certain polymer grades and applications. Verify requirements for your specific product and end-use before placing orders.
