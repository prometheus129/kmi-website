## Why Converters Are Switching POM Suppliers

Sourcing POM (polyoxymethylene) from Chinese producers offers 20-28% cost reduction compared to Western and Japanese incumbents — with no quality compromise at the leading producers. POM, commonly called acetal, is the default material for precision mechanical parts that need low friction, excellent fatigue resistance, and tight dimensional tolerance: gears, bearings, actuators, clips, springs, cam mechanisms, and conveyor components.

The market has been dominated by Western suppliers for decades: Celanese (Hostaform copolymer), TJC LP (Delrin homopolymer — acquired from DuPont's engineering polymers portfolio in November 2023), and BASF (Ultraform). Polyplastics (Duracon) holds the dominant position in Japan and Southeast Asia.

China is now the world's largest POM producer by volume. The feedstock advantage is structural: POM is made from formaldehyde, derived from methanol, which in China is overwhelmingly produced from coal gasification. The entire POM feedstock chain is domestic and coal-based — near-zero exposure to imported naphtha or natural gas pricing. This produces a cost advantage that persists regardless of oil price cycles. ## Homopolymer vs. Copolymer: The First Procurement Decision

POM exists in two fundamentally different polymer types. This is not a grade variation — it is a different chemistry with different properties. Specifying the wrong type creates application failures that no amount of processing optimization can fix.

### POM Homopolymer

The legacy Delrin material (now owned by TJC LP following the November 2023 acquisition) is the reference homopolymer. Higher crystallinity delivers 5-10% higher tensile strength, better fatigue resistance, superior creep resistance, and higher HDT than copolymer at equivalent molecular weight.

**Trade-offs:** Less thermally stable during processing — generates formaldehyde if overheated, creating workplace safety and mold deposit issues. Narrower processing window. Less resistant to hot water and alkaline environments.

**When to specify:** High-load gears, structural clips under sustained stress, bearings under high PV conditions. If the incumbent specification calls for "Delrin" or "homopolymer," the replacement must also be homopolymer.

### POM Copolymer

A copolymer of trioxane with a small amount of comonomer (typically ethylene oxide or 1,3-dioxolane). Hostaform, Ultraform, and Duracon are all copolymer POM.

**Advantages:** Better thermal stability during processing — wider processing window, less formaldehyde emission, fewer mold deposits. Better resistance to hot water and mildly alkaline environments.

**When to specify:** Most POM applications use copolymer today because the processing advantages outweigh the modest mechanical penalty. Automotive clips, consumer product mechanisms, plumbing fittings, food contact components, and any high-volume production application.

**Procurement implication:** The majority of Chinese POM production is copolymer. If your application requires homopolymer POM, verify that the Chinese supplier produces homopolymer — not all do. If your application uses copolymer POM (Hostaform, Duracon, Ultraform), Chinese copolymer alternatives are widely available and well-validated.

## POM Formaldehyde Emission: Regulatory Requirements for Acetal Compounds

Formaldehyde emission from POM is a regulatory and quality concern in several application segments:

**Automotive interior:** All major automotive OEMs test formaldehyde emissions from interior components per VDA 275 or similar standards. Low-emission POM grades are available from both Western and Chinese producers — specify the emission requirement explicitly.

**Food contact:** FDA and EU food contact regulations limit formaldehyde migration from POM. If your application is food contact, verify the supplier's compliance documentation (FDA 21 CFR 177.2470 for POM specifically).

**Procurement implication:** When requesting quotes, specify your formaldehyde emission requirement (VDA 275 result below X mg/kg, or FDA food contact compliant). Low-emission formulations use additional stabilization and scavenger systems that add modest cost but ensure compliance.

## Qualification Protocol for POM

### Step 1: Polymer Type Match

Confirm homopolymer or copolymer matches the incumbent. This is non-negotiable — the types are not interchangeable.

Verification: request DSC (differential scanning calorimetry) data. Homopolymer POM melts at ~175°C with a sharp peak. Copolymer melts at ~165°C with a broader peak. This is a definitive test.

### Step 2: Grade-Level Property Comparison

| Property | Typical Copolymer POM | What to Verify |
|----------|----------------------|----------------|
| Tensile strength (at yield) | 62-68 MPa | Match incumbent within 5% |
| Flexural modulus | 2.6-2.9 GPa | Match incumbent within 5% |
| Impact (notched Izod, 23°C) | 55-75 J/m | Critical for clips, springs |
| Impact (notched Izod, -30°C) | 40-55 J/m | Automotive requirement |
| HDT at 1.8 MPa | 95-110°C | Application temperature limit |
| Melt flow rate (190°C / 2.16 kg) | 8-12 g/10min (standard flow) | Different MFR = different flow behavior |
| Coefficient of friction | 0.20-0.35 (POM on steel) | Critical for gear and bearing applications |
| Specific gravity | 1.41 (copolymer), 1.42 (homopolymer) | Deviation indicates filler or different polymer |

### Step 3: Friction and Wear Testing

For gear, bearing, and sliding contact applications, datasheet properties are insufficient. Request:
- **PV limit data** — the maximum pressure x velocity product before thermal failure
- **Wear rate** — mm3/Nm or mg/km under specified conditions
- **Coefficient of friction** — static and dynamic, against your specific mating surface
- Run wear testing on your actual part geometry if possible — test bar data does not capture the thermal and contact conditions of your application

### Step 4: Processing Trial

- **Shrinkage:** POM has relatively high shrinkage (~2.0-2.5% for copolymer). The exact shrinkage differs between compounds — measure on your parts and adjust dimensions if needed.
- **Mold deposit:** POM generates formaldehyde and oligomers that deposit on mold surfaces. Different compounds produce different deposit rates. Run 1,000+ shots and evaluate mold maintenance frequency.
- **Barrel residence time:** POM degrades if it sits in the barrel too long at processing temperature. Verify the compound's thermal stability window.

### Step 5: Application-Specific Validation

- **Gear testing:** Run accelerated gear life testing at the application condition. Gear life depends on fatigue endurance, heat generation at the mesh point, and dimensional stability under load.
- **Snap fit retention:** For POM clips and fasteners, measure insertion and extraction force. Spring-back behavior depends on molecular weight and crystallinity — different compounds may produce different retention forces.
- **Chemical exposure:** If contact with fuels, coolants, or cleaning agents, verify dimensional and property stability after exposure at temperature.

## Warning Signs When Evaluating Chinese POM Suppliers

Watch for these warning signs during qualification:

**1. Homopolymer sold as copolymer (or vice versa).** If the supplier cannot provide DSC data clearly showing the melting point (175°C for homopolymer, 165°C for copolymer), the polymer type is unverified. Supplying the wrong type in production will cause application failures.

**2. High formaldehyde emission without disclosure.** If the supplier cannot provide VDA 275 test results or does not offer low-emission grades, their standard POM may not meet automotive interior requirements. This is not a quality defect — it is a formulation choice. But it must be specified upfront, not discovered after molding.

**3. Inconsistent MFR between batches.** Melt flow rate variation greater than +/- 15% between lots indicates inconsistent polymerization or blending. Request COAs from 3-5 consecutive production lots and check MFR consistency.

**4. No friction or wear data available.** For gear and bearing applications, a supplier that cannot provide PV limit, wear rate, or coefficient of friction data has not validated their POM for mechanical applications. Datasheet tensile and flexural properties alone are insufficient for these use cases.

## When POM Is — and Isn't — the Right Material

**POM is the right choice when:**
- The application requires low friction, high fatigue endurance, or spring-like behavior
- Dimensional precision is critical (gears, bearings, mechanisms)
- The operating environment includes hydrocarbons or solvents
- Self-lubrication is needed (POM's low friction coefficient against metals and itself)

**Consider alternatives when:**
- Operating temperature exceeds 100°C continuously (consider PA66 or [PPS](/insights/pps-polyphenylene-sulfide-china-ev-battery-automotive))
- Strong acids or bases are present (POM has poor resistance)
- Flame retardancy is required (POM is inherently flammable — UL 94 HB only; FR-POM grades exist but are limited). See our guide to [halogen-free FR polymers from China](/insights/halogen-free-flame-retardant-polymers-china-certified).
- High stiffness is needed (POM flexural modulus of ~2.7 GPa is modest compared to [GF-reinforced polymers](/insights/glass-fiber-reinforced-polymers-china-gf-compounds) at 8-16 GPa)
- Electrical insulation with high CTI is required (consider [PBT](/insights/pbt-polybutylene-terephthalate-china-connectors-housings) which offers CTI up to 600V vs. POM's poor tracking resistance)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can Chinese POM replace Delrin homopolymer in existing applications?**
Yes, provided the Chinese supplier produces homopolymer POM — not all do, as the majority of Chinese POM capacity is copolymer. Verify with DSC data (homopolymer melts at ~175°C vs. ~165°C for copolymer) and confirm key properties match within 5% of the Delrin grade you are replacing. For high-load gears and structural clips, homopolymer is not optional — copolymer substitution will result in reduced fatigue life and creep resistance.

**What is the actual cost saving when switching POM from Western to Chinese suppliers?**
At current market pricing, Chinese POM copolymer is 20-28% below Western alternatives on a CFR Southeast Asia basis. For a converter processing 20 MT/month, this translates to $96,000-168,000 in annual material cost reduction. **How do I qualify Chinese POM for food contact applications?**
Verify the supplier provides FDA 21 CFR 177.2470 compliance documentation and, for EU markets, a Declaration of Conformity per Regulation (EU) No 10/2011. Additionally, request formaldehyde migration test results specific to your application conditions (temperature, contact time, food simulant). Low-emission POM formulations with additional stabilizers are available from leading Chinese producers.

**Is Chinese POM consistent enough for high-volume automotive production?**
The leading Chinese POM producers operate continuous polymerization processes with statistical process control infrastructure validated for automotive-grade consistency. Chinese POM is in volume production at major automotive OEMs and Tier 1 suppliers across China. The key qualification step is verifying lot-to-lot consistency — request COAs from multiple consecutive production lots and check MFR, tensile, and impact variation.

**What is the minimum order quantity for Chinese POM?**
For initial qualification, most Chinese POM producers accept trial orders of 500 kg-1 MT. Sample quantities of 5-25 kg for testing are typically available with 2-4 week lead time. For ongoing production supply, MOQs vary by grade but 5-10 MT per order is common for standard copolymer grades.
