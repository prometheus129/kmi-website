## The Deal Happens in the Documents

India customs clearance doesn't care what you paid, negotiated, or scheduled. It cares what's on the Bill of Entry, whether the Certificate of Origin corresponds to actual production, whether the HS code matches the grade claimed, and whether the Certificate of Analysis matches the commercial invoice. Get any one of those wrong on a preferential rate claim, and the goods either clear at the standard MFN rate with a duty shortfall penalty, or sit at the port accruing demurrage while the paperwork gets reconciled.

For mid-tier Indian polymer importers running 20-40 containers per year, documentation error costs typically translate to $2,400-10,800/year in demurrage, duty reassessment, and broker fees — entirely avoidable with pre-shipment verification. This article walks through what to check.

> **Quick summary for the scanner:**
> - **Documents required:** Bill of Entry (ICEGATE), Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Bill of Lading, Certificate of Analysis, MSDS, Insurance Certificate, GSTIN on BoE.
> - **Certificate of Origin needed** only for preferential rate claims: APTA Certificate (China — partial BCD preference via CCPIT), Form AI (ASEAN — 0% BCD via AIFTA), India-Korea CEPA Certificate, India-UAE CEPA Certificate (TRQ-based).

## The Document Set Required for India Polymer Imports

Core set for every polymer shipment entering India:

1. **Bill of Entry (BoE)** — filed electronically through ICEGATE. Primary import declaration. Must include correct HSN code, CIF value, GSTIN, description matching commercial invoice.
2. **Commercial Invoice** — from exporter. Must show grade, HS code, unit price, total value, payment terms, incoterm (CIF/FOB/CFR). Third-party invoicing (issuer different from manufacturer) requires additional documentation chain.
3. **Packing List** — weight, quantity, container number, seal details. Must match commercial invoice and CoA batch/lot numbers.
4. **Bill of Lading** (ocean) or Airway Bill (air) — transport document. Issued by carrier. Consignee and notify party must match importer records.
5. **Certificate of Analysis (CoA)** — grade specifications: melt flow index (MFI), density, isotactic index (PP), intrinsic viscosity (PET), molecular weight or RV (polyamides), additive package description. Batch/lot number must match packing list.
6. **Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS/SDS)** — hazard classifications, handling and storage requirements. Required for customs clearance regardless of origin preference.
7. **Insurance Certificate** — marine cargo insurance at CIF value minimum. Required for assessable value determination.
8. **GSTIN declared on Bill of Entry** — mandatory for claiming IGST input tax credit. Absence causes IGST to become an unrecoverable cost.

**Conditional documents** (required only when preferential tariff is claimed):

9. **Certificate of Origin (CoO)** — format depends on origin preference (see next section).
10. **IEC (Importer Exporter Code) from DGFT** — one-time registration requirement, not per-shipment document.
11. **BIS certification** — no longer required for LDPE, LLDPE, HDPE, PP, PVC, ABS, or PC in primary form following the November 12, 2025 rescission of 14 BIS Quality Control Orders. See [India Polymer Market 2026](/insights/india-polymer-market-2026-bis-antidumping-china-import) for full context.

## HS Code Classification — Where Most Clearance Delays Start

HS misclassification is the single largest documentation risk for preferential rate claims. Customs assessment compares declared HS against shipment description and CoA; a mismatch triggers reclassification, duty reassessment, and potentially penalty.

### Polyethylene HS Code by Density

| PE Grade | Specific Gravity | HS Code | Typical Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLDPE | < 0.94 | 3901.10.92 | Packaging film, stretch film |
| LDPE | < 0.94 | 3901.10.xx | Film, liners, coatings |
| HDPE | ≥ 0.94 | 3901.20.00 or 3901.90 | Blow molding, pipe, film |
| mLLDPE | < 0.94 | 3901.10.xx | High-performance film (metallocene) |

**The density boundary at 0.94 is a common customs audit point.** If CoA shows density 0.935 and importer declared HS 3901.20 (HDPE), customs will reclassify to 3901.10 (LLDPE/LDPE) — changing both applicable BCD and potential preferential rate eligibility. Always cross-check CoA density against the HS code on the Bill of Entry before filing.

### Polypropylene Homopolymer vs Copolymer

- **PP homopolymer** (e.g., T30S raffia, most injection grades): HS 3902.10
- **PP copolymer** (random copolymer, impact copolymer): HS 3902.30
- **PP block copolymer** (automotive grade, bumper compounds): HS 3902.30 or specific subheading

The homo/copoly distinction affects preferential rate eligibility under AIFTA and CEPA. Some HS 3902.10 lines have stronger preferential rates than 3902.30, or vice versa depending on the specific agreement. Misclassifying automotive PP impact copolymer as "PP" without the copoly subheading is a common error.

### Compound Grades vs Base Resin

| Grade Type | Typical HS | Preferential Rate Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Virgin unfilled PE/PP/PA resin | 3901/3902/3908 primary subheadings | Preferential rate typically available |
| GF-reinforced compound (PA66-GF30, PBT-GF30) | HS 3901.90 "other," HS 3907.99, or specific compound subheadings | Often outside preferential rate scope — verify per-notification |
| Mineral-filled compounds | HS 3901.90, 3902.90, or specific compound headings | Verify scope |
| Pigmented masterbatch | HS 3901.90, 3902.90, or 3206 series (color concentrates) | Frequently outside preferential scope |
| PC/ABS alloys | HS 3903.30 (if ABS-dominant), 3907.40 (PC-dominant), or compound heading | Verify per grade |

**Key risk:** A PA66-GF30 compound from Shenma or Remay classified under a mixture heading not named in Notification No. 12/2026-Customs loses the nil-BCD benefit during the April-June window. Similarly, an automotive PP impact copolymer classified as HS 3902.90 "other" rather than 3902.30 may lose AIFTA preference post-window. Compound HS verification must happen before filing, not after customs query.

### PVC Suspension vs Paste — Different Duty Treatment

- **PVC suspension resin (S-PVC, SG-5, SG-7)**: HS 3904.10 — no active ADD from China
- **PVC paste resin (e-PVC, E-PVC)**: HS 3904.40 — **active anti-dumping duty up to $707/MT from China** under Notification No. 09/2024-Customs ADD

Misclassifying Chinese PVC paste as suspension (or vice versa) has direct duty-payment consequences. The distinction between suspension and paste process at the CoA / producer specification level is unambiguous, but the classification risk comes from generic "PVC resin" descriptions on commercial invoices — always require exporter to specify suspension or paste on commercial invoice and CoA.

### PET Grade Classification

- **PET resin (bottle, preform, film grades)**: HS 3907.61 — covered by nil-BCD window
- **Recycled PET (rPET)**: HS 3907.61 if primary form, 3915 for waste/scrap — different rate treatment

## Certificates of Origin by Preferential Framework

During the April 2 - June 30, 2026 nil-BCD window, **no Certificate of Origin is required for preferential rate** — BCD is zero for all origins under Notification No. 12/2026-Customs. If the window closes July 1 (see [Q3 post-window pricing models](/insights/india-polymer-q3-2026-post-window-pricing-models)), CoO becomes essential again for claiming preferential rates.

### APTA Certificate of Origin (Chinese Origin)

**Asia-Pacific Trade Agreement** — both India and China are members. Certificate issued by the **China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT)**. Fourth Round concessions effective July 1, 2018 provide a margin of preference on selected polymer tariff lines — typically a partial BCD reduction (roughly 1-2 percentage points) rather than full elimination. Not all polymer HS codes are in scope; product-specific rates require verification against the APTA schedule at the Indian Trade Portal.

**Practical workflow for Chinese origin:**
- Chinese exporter applies to CCPIT for APTA CoO before shipment
- Certificate references specific HS code and product description matching commercial invoice
- CCPIT-stamped original (not copy) presented at Indian customs with Bill of Entry
- Importer claims APTA rate on BoE; customs verifies against certificate

### Form AI (ASEAN-India FTA)

**ASEAN-India Free Trade Agreement** — applies to origins from Thailand, Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam, Philippines, and other ASEAN member states. Form AI provides **0% BCD on most PE and PP tariff lines** with proper documentation — the most favorable preferential treatment available for polymer imports into India after China-APTA and Korea-CEPA.

Issuing authority varies by country:
- **Thailand**: Department of Foreign Trade (DFT)
- **Singapore**: Singapore Customs
- **Indonesia**: Ministry of Trade (Kementerian Perdagangan)
- **Malaysia**: Ministry of International Trade and Industry (MITI)

**Rules of Origin requirements:** Regional Value Content (RVC) threshold OR change in tariff classification (CTC). For polymers, CTC from monomer HS (chapter 39 feedstock) to polymer HS is typically satisfied by polymerization within the origin country. A **10th round of AIFTA Rules of Origin renegotiation was held August 2025** — RoO tightening is under discussion and could affect future Form AI application.

### India-Korea CEPA 2.0

**India-Korea Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement** — revised CEPA 2.0 framework in effect enforces stricter Rules of Origin than the original 2010 framework. Preferential BCD rates on selected polymer tariff lines for Korean origin (LG Chem, Lotte Chemical, SK Geo Centric, Hanwha Solutions) — typically graduated preference (roughly 2-5% effective BCD depending on HS code), not full 0% elimination.

- Certificate of Origin issued by authorized Korean agency
- Rules of Origin typically require change in tariff subheading (CTSH) or RVC threshold
- **Korea CEPA has meaningful preferential rates on ABS (HS 3903.30)** — relevant for buyers considering ABS origin diversification post-nil-BCD window

### India-UAE CEPA (Tariff Rate Quota Scheme)

**India-UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement** — operates as a Tariff Rate Quota (TRQ) on polymers, not simple graduated preference:

- **Within annual quota**: 0% BCD (LDPE cap approximately 50,000 MT/yr; similar caps on other polymer lines)
- **Above quota**: MFN rate (7.5% BCD + 0.75% SWS)

Borouge Ruwais (Abu Dhabi) is the primary UAE polymer exporter. Before committing volume to UAE origin, **verify quota utilization status** via DGFT's India-UAE CEPA Public Notice — late in the fiscal year, quotas may be fully allocated and new imports face full MFN rate.

Note: Borouge Ruwais is Hormuz-exposed — during ongoing Strait of Hormuz disruption, UAE-origin reliability is degraded regardless of tariff treatment.

### Proof of Origin Enforcement — Circular No. 14/2025-Customs

**Circular No. 14/2025-Customs effective March 18, 2025** replaced "Certificate of Origin" with "Proof of Origin" under CAROTAR 2020. The enforcement change means preferential origin certificates must correspond to actual production in the claimed origin country — the certificate alone is not sufficient if the evidence chain is weak.

**Practical implications for documentation:**
- Commercial invoice issuer (if third-party) must align with manufacturer on CoO
- Mill test reports, production records, or equivalent documentation may be requested by customs
- Third-country routing via ASEAN (Chinese polymer exported through Bangkok under Form AI) is being actively policed
- Form AI on product actually manufactured at Chinese source = compliance risk, not tariff strategy
- Legitimate dual-sourcing with genuine Thai, Korean, or UAE mill = fine
- Trading intermediaries using ASEAN-based entities to reroute Chinese production = risk of reassessment + penalty

For Indian importers planning post-window origin diversification, supplier relationships must be with actual producing mills, not trading companies sourcing from multiple origins.

## Certificate of Analysis (CoA) — What to Verify Pre-Shipment

CoA verification catches most downstream quality and compliance problems before they reach Indian customs or the buyer's production line. Five primary checks:

### 1. Grade Name and Producer Match
Grade on CoA must match grade specified on PO and commercial invoice. Mismatches (e.g., PO specifies "HDPE 5502AA" but CoA shows "HDPE 5502GA") trigger customs query and potential reassessment. Producer plant identifier on CoA should match the claimed supply source — important for CEPA/AIFTA proof of origin and for IATF 16949-sensitive automotive grades.

### 2. Key Property Ranges Within Specification
- **MFI** — melt flow index for thermoplastics, must fall within PO tolerance
- **Density** — for PE grades, density is the HS code determinant; declared density on BoE must match CoA
- **Isotactic index** — for PP grades, affects tensile and tape performance
- **Intrinsic viscosity** — for PET, determines processing window for bottle/preform applications
- **Molecular weight or relative viscosity (RV)** — for polyamides, determines engineering vs fiber grade (RV&lt;3 fiber, 2.7-3.5 engineering)
- **Ash content, volatiles** — for impurity-sensitive applications

### 3. Batch or Lot Number Consistency
Batch number on CoA must match packing list and commercial invoice. Variance between CoA sample batch and actual shipment batch causes downstream processing issues even if clearance proceeds.

### 4. Date of Analysis
CoA issued months before shipment may not reflect actual batch properties. Request CoA dated within 30 days of production for each specific shipment, not a generic grade-level CoA.

### 5. Compliance Statements (Application-Specific)
- **Food-contact grades**: FDA 21 CFR 177.1520, EU 10/2011, BIS IS 10146 compliance
- **Medical grades**: USP Class VI, ISO 10993 biocompatibility
- **Electrical grades**: UL 94 flame rating (V-0, V-1, V-2), dielectric properties
- **Automotive grades**: IATF 16949 plant certification, specific OEM approvals (Maruti, Hyundai, Tata, Mahindra AVL)
- **REACH compliance**: for exports to EU markets

For pharma and medical-device polymer imports, additional pharmacopeia documentation beyond CoA is typically required.

## Common Errors That Get Caught at JNPT, Mundra, Chennai

Top 10 documentation errors Indian customs typically flags:

1. **HS code mismatch with CoA properties** — PE density indicating one HS, declared BoE HS indicating another
2. **Preferential rate claim without valid CoO** — BoE claims APTA/AIFTA/CEPA rate but certificate not presented or improperly issued
3. **Third-party invoicing without supporting documentation** — invoice issuer differs from manufacturer, proof-of-origin evidence chain weak
4. **PVC suspension vs paste misdescription** — generic "PVC resin" on invoice fails to distinguish, defaults to higher-duty classification
5. **Compound grade classified as base resin** — PA66-GF30 declared as HS 3908.10 (base PA66) rather than compound subheading, preferential rate claim rejected
6. **CoA batch mismatch with packing list** — batch on CoA differs from batch in container, customs query triggered
7. **Missing GSTIN on Bill of Entry** — IGST becomes unrecoverable cost, not just delayed credit
8. **Inadequate MSDS for hazard-classified polymers** — some polymer grades require specific hazard classification documentation
9. **Insurance certificate missing or CIF value inconsistent** — assessable value determination challenged
10. **Proof-of-origin evidence insufficient under CAROTAR** — since March 18, 2025, customs can require production records beyond certificate

The common thread: most errors are preventable pre-shipment. Post-import dispute resolution costs demurrage, duty reassessment, potential penalty, and broker fees.

## FAQs

### What documents are required for polymer imports into India?

Bill of Entry (ICEGATE-filed), Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Bill of Lading or Airway Bill, Certificate of Analysis, MSDS/SDS, Insurance Certificate, and GSTIN on BoE. Certificate of Origin required only for preferential rate claims (APTA for China, Form AI for ASEAN, India-Korea CEPA, India-UAE CEPA). During nil-BCD window April 2 - June 30, 2026, no CoO required because BCD is 0% for all origins.

### Which HS codes apply to Chinese polymer imports?

PE: LLDPE/LDPE at HS 3901.10 (density less than 0.94), HDPE at 3901.20 or 3901.90 (density at least 0.94). PP: homopolymer HS 3902.10, copolymer 3902.30. PVC: suspension 3904.10, paste 3904.40. PET: 3907.61. PC: 3907.40. POM: 3907.10. PBT: 3907.99. PA6/PA66: 3908.10. PS: 3903.11. ABS: 3903.30. Compound grades may classify under different subheadings than base resin.

### What is proof of origin and how is it different from certificate of origin?

Proof of Origin (enforced via Circular No. 14/2025-Customs effective March 18, 2025) requires that a preferential origin certificate correspond to actual production in the claimed origin country. Certificate alone is not sufficient if evidence chain is weak. Customs can request production records, mill test reports, or equivalent documentation. Third-country routing (Chinese polymer exported through ASEAN under Form AI) is being actively policed.

### What should I verify on a Certificate of Analysis before shipment?

Five checks: (1) grade name and producer plant match PO and CoO; (2) key properties (MFI, density, isotactic index, intrinsic viscosity, RV) within PO specification; (3) batch/lot number matches packing list; (4) date of analysis within 30 days of production; (5) compliance statements present for application (food-contact, medical, automotive).

### What are the most common HS classification errors?

(1) PE density misclassification at the 0.94 SG boundary. (2) PP homo vs copoly confusion. (3) Compound grades classified as base resin. (4) PVC suspension vs paste generic "PVC resin" descriptions. (5) Third-party invoicing misalignment with CoO. Most errors are preventable with pre-shipment verification.

### What does documentation error typically cost?

$2,400-10,800/year typical range for mid-tier importers running 20-40 containers through preferential rate claims. Components: demurrage ($15-40/container/day after free period), detention ($20-30/container/day), duty reassessment at MFN rate, penalty up to 100% of shortfall, CFS storage, broker fees, legal costs. A single failed preferential rate claim on a 14-day hold can easily cost $800-1,500 per container before business impact.

### Do I need BIS certification for polymer imports?

No, for LDPE, LLDPE, HDPE, PP, PVC, ABS, EVA, PU, or PC in primary form — these BIS Quality Control Orders were rescinded on November 12, 2025. Engineering polymer resins (PA6, PA66, POM, PBT) in primary form were not previously subject to mandatory BIS either. Specific finished articles may have separate product-specific standards. Net effect: no mandatory BIS barrier for polymer resin imports in April 2026.

### How does India-UAE CEPA TRQ affect my UAE-origin imports?

India-UAE CEPA imposes Tariff Rate Quotas on polymers — 0% BCD within annual quota (LDPE cap approximately 50,000 MT/yr), MFN 7.5% + SWS above quota. Verify quota utilization status via DGFT's CEPA Public Notice before committing volume; late-year imports may face full MFN rate if quota exhausted. Borouge Ruwais is the primary UAE polymer exporter, but Hormuz disruption affects reliability regardless of tariff treatment.
