> **Summary:** Chinese HDPE grade codes are not strictly standardized — order by application and verified specification, not by the code alone. 5000S is primarily a raffia / monofilament grade (low melt flow). 5502 is a low-melt-flow blow molding grade. "7260" in Western trade is Hostalen GC 7260, an injection grade (MFI ~8) — not a film grade. All figures below are indicative; confirm against the producer COA/TDS for your lot.

## The Grade-Code Problem That Costs Money

You order "HDPE 5000S" from your Chinese trader expecting a blow molding grade for your bottle line. The container arrives, you check the COA, and the melt flow is far lower than a blow grade should run — because the dominant 5000S in trade is a raffia / monofilament grade, not a blow molding grade. Or you order "7260" against a Western drawing that called for Hostalen GC 7260 and receive something nowhere near MFI 8. The grade code did not mean what you assumed.

This is the core hazard in Chinese HDPE procurement: **grade codes are not strictly standardized across producers, and a base number can map to genuinely different products.** The fix is not memorizing a code table — it is ordering by application and verified specification (MFI, density, ESCR), and requiring a COA before shipment.

This guide breaks down three HDPE grade codes that buyers in Vietnam routinely encounter — 5000S, 5502, and "7260" — by what they actually are, and how to specify what you need correctly. Every figure here is indicative; confirm against the producer's COA/TDS for your lot.

## What Are HDPE 5000S, 5502, and "7260"?

| | HDPE 5000S | HDPE 5502 | "7260" (Hostalen GC 7260) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it actually is** | Raffia / monofilament grade | Low-melt-flow blow molding grade | Injection molding grade (LyondellBasell) |
| **Primary application** | Woven sacks, rope, nets, monofilament | Bottles, jerrycans, drums | Crates, housewares, pails, toys |
| **MFI (190°C/2.16kg)** | ~0.8 g/10min | ~0.25-0.35 g/10min | ~8 g/10min |
| **Density** | ~0.953 g/cm³ | ~0.954-0.955 g/cm³ | ~0.960 g/cm³ |
| **Why buyers confuse it** | Code is sometimes applied loosely to other grades | Low melt flow needed for parison strength | Shares the bare number "7260" with other listings |

The table above is the single most useful correction in this guide: **5000S is not a blow molding grade, and "7260" in Western trade is an injection grade, not a film grade.** Both assumptions are common and both lead to the wrong material on the line.

**Why melt flow matters in blow molding:** the polymer hangs as a molten tube (parison) that must support its own weight before the mold closes. Lower melt flow = higher molecular weight = stronger melt = the parison does not sag. That is why a blow molding grade like 5502 runs at a low MFI (~0.25-0.35), while an injection grade like GC 7260 runs much higher (~8) because it must fill a mold under pressure, not hang as a self-supporting parison.

## HDPE 5000S: What Is It and When Should You Use It?

**What it actually is:** Primarily a raffia / monofilament grade — a low-melt-flow HDPE used for woven sacks, rope, nets, and monofilament, not blow-molded containers. The single most common mistake with this code is assuming "5000S" means a bottle/jerrycan blow grade; the dominant 5000S in trade is not.

**Indicative specifications (confirm against the producer COA/TDS for your lot — these are not from a single producer's published datasheet):**

| Property | Indicative Value |
|---|---|
| MFI (190°C/2.16kg) | ~0.8 g/10min |
| Density | ~0.953 g/cm³ |
| Primary application | Raffia, monofilament — woven sacks, rope, nets |

**Why melt flow is low for raffia:** the molten polymer is drawn into tapes or filaments that must hold tensile strength without breaking. A low MFI (higher molecular weight) gives the tape the draw-down strength it needs. This is a different reason for low MFI than blow molding's parison strength — but it explains why 5000S is not interchangeable with a higher-flow film or injection grade.

**When 5000S is the right grade:**
- Woven polypropylene-style HDPE sacks, raffia tape, rope, agricultural and fishing nets, monofilament

**If you actually need a blow molding grade and were quoted "5000S":** stop and verify the application and MFI on the producer's TDS. Order by application and target specification, not by the code. For blow molding, see 5502 below.

## HDPE 5502: The Blow Molding Grade

HDPE 5502 is a **low-melt-flow blow molding grade** — a single grade, not a family of injection-versus-blow variants. There is no reliable "AA = blow / GA = injection" suffix split for this code; do not order on the assumption that the suffix tells you the process. Order by application, MFI, density, and ESCR.

**Indicative specifications (confirm against the producer COA/TDS for your lot):**

| Property | Indicative Value |
|---|---|
| MFI (190°C/2.16kg) | ~0.25-0.35 g/10min |
| Density | ~0.954-0.955 g/cm³ |
| Primary application | Bottles, jerrycans, drums (blow molding) |
| ESCR (F50, ASTM D1693) | Varies by producer and lot — specify a minimum on the PO |

**Why the low melt flow:** in blow molding the polymer hangs as a molten parison that must support its own weight before the mold closes. The low MFI gives the parison melt strength so it does not sag — which is exactly what a large bottle or jerrycan needs.

**When to specify 5502:**
- Bottles, jerrycans, and drums where parison melt strength matters
- Chemical containers — but ESCR is the binding property and it varies by producer and lot. Do not assume a number; specify a minimum F50 (ASTM D1693) on the PO and require ESCR data on the COA. For the most aggressive contents (concentrated detergents, solvents, agrochemicals), a bimodal grade may be required.

**How to order HDPE blow molding grades without surprises:**
1. Specify the application explicitly — "blow molding, jerrycan, 20L" — not just the code
2. Specify a target MFI with tolerance — e.g., "MFI ~0.3 ±0.05" — not "low MFI"
3. Specify an ESCR minimum where the contents are aggressive, and require ESCR on the COA
4. Require a COA before shipment and check it against your line's calibration window

## Is "7260" the Same as Hostalen GC 7260?

In Western trade, **"7260" means Hostalen GC 7260 (LyondellBasell) — an injection molding grade**, not a film or blow molding grade. This is the referent that matters when you are matching a Western drawing or specification.

| Hostalen GC 7260 (LyondellBasell) | |
|---|---|
| MFI (190°C/2.16kg) | ~8 g/10min |
| Density | ~0.960 g/cm³ |
| Application | Injection — crates, housewares, pails, toys |
| Process | Injection molding |

If you are replacing a Western specification that calls for "Hostalen GC 7260," you need a **Chinese injection grade at MFI ~8 and density ~0.960** — not a low-melt-flow blow or raffia grade, and not a film grade. The number alone is not enough; match the MFI, density, and process, and pilot-test before committing volume.

**A note on what this guide does NOT claim:** there is no reliably confirmable native Chinese "7260" film grade or pipe grade that we will assert specifications for here. If a trader offers a Chinese "7260," treat the bare number as ambiguous and pin down application, MFI, and density on the TDS before ordering.

**Related Chinese HDPE film grades to know (for film applications):**
- **DGDB 6097** (PetroChina Daqing) — one of the two most-traded Chinese HDPE film grades in Southeast Asia (alongside Sinopec 6095). Standard MFI (I2, 190C/2.16kg) approximately 0.05-0.18 g/10min; HLMI (21.6kg) approximately 7-9 g/10min. Density ~0.946-0.952. Primary substitute for Q-Chem TR-144 and SABIC F00952.
- **Sinopec 6095** (Yanshan) — the other of the two most-traded Chinese HDPE film grades in SE Asia. Similar specification to DGDB 6097, sometimes used interchangeably in trade
- **HDPE 9255** — higher MFI (~2.5 per trade sources, verify COA), higher output on fast blown film lines. Used for shopping bags, light-duty packaging, agricultural film.

**One quality difference Vietnamese film converters should know:** Chinese HDPE film grades are predominantly unimodal (single-reactor process). Korean and Saudi equivalents (Q-Chem TR-144, SABIC F00952) are often bimodal (dual-reactor process), which produces higher dart drop impact strength — approximately 5-10% higher per industry testing. For basic packaging film, this difference is immaterial. For heavy-duty sacks or stretch-hood applications, it may affect performance.

## How Are HDPE Grades Priced Relative to Each Other?

HDPE grades in China follow a broadly predictable pricing structure based on specification difficulty:

- **Film grades** (9255, DGDB 6097, 6095) — typically the **lowest price tier.** Commodity volumes, many producers, competitive market.
- **Raffia / monofilament grades** (5000S) — commodity-priced, high-volume, many producers.
- **Blow molding grades** (5502 and similar low-melt-flow grades) — often a **modest premium** above commodity film, especially where a tighter ESCR specification narrows the qualifying producer pool. The premium varies with market conditions.
- **Injection grades** (e.g., GC 7260 equivalents at MFI ~8) — generally commodity-priced; high volume, many producers.

The hierarchy reflects production economics: tighter specs = lower yield rates = higher per-ton cost. For buyers sourcing multiple HDPE grades, understanding this hierarchy prevents overpaying for commodity-spec product and underpaying (and underspecifying) for grades that need to perform. Treat the tiering as directional, not a fixed price ladder — relative pricing moves with feedstock and market conditions.

## What to Specify on Your Purchase Order

Because Chinese HDPE grade codes are not strictly standardized, every HDPE purchase order to a Chinese supplier should specify the requirement, not just the code:

1. **Application stated explicitly** — "blow molding, 20L jerrycan" — so the supplier cannot ship a different-process grade under the same number
2. **Target MFI with acceptable range** — "MFI ~0.3 +/-0.05" not "low MFI"
3. **Target density** — confirms you're getting the right grade family
4. **ESCR requirement where it matters** — "F50 >= [your minimum] per ASTM D1693" for chemical-contact blow molding; require ESCR data on the COA
5. **COA before shipment** — compare every batch against your spec before it loads
6. **Producer/plant name** — "Sinopec Yangzi" not "Sinopec" (different plants, different consistency)

## Common Processing Issues with Chinese HDPE Grades

| Symptom on Your Line | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Parison sag (blow molding — bottle walls too thin at top) | MFI too high for your mold size | Switch to a lower-MFI blow molding grade (e.g., 5502 at ~0.25-0.35) |
| Container stress cracking in service | ESCR too low for the contents | Check ESCR on COA; specify a higher F50 minimum or step up to a bimodal grade |
| Film gels or fish-eyes | Contamination, or a blow/raffia grade on a film line by mistake | Verify the grade is a film type (DGDB 6097, 6095), not a blow or raffia grade |
| Inconsistent wall thickness (blow molding) | New batch from a different Sinopec/PetroChina plant | Run trial batch; adjust parison programming for new MFI |
| Poor film tear resistance | Unimodal Chinese HDPE vs. bimodal Korean/Saudi grade | Expected — approximately 5-10% lower dart drop impact; adjust gauge or accept trade-off |
| Die swell variation between shipments | Different catalyst system (ZN vs. chrome) at different plants | Specify plant on PO; or adjust die temperature +/-5°C per batch |

## How Consistent Are Chinese HDPE Grades Between Plants?

Chinese HDPE grades can show MFI variation of +/-10-15% between different Sinopec/PetroChina plants producing the same grade designation. This is partly because different subsidiaries may use different catalyst systems (Ziegler-Natta vs. chrome) and reactor configurations (slurry vs. gas phase), producing the same nominal grade with subtly different molecular weight distributions.

**Practical impact for Vietnamese converters:** You may notice differences in die swell, melt fracture onset, and cooling behavior between the same grade from different Chinese plants. This doesn't mean the product is defective — it means you need to tune your process for each source. The first run from a new producer should always be a trial batch, not a full production commitment.

Every plant, every batch, every reactor configuration produces subtly different material. Knowing which Sinopec subsidiary made your blow molding grade — and whether Daqing's DGDB 6097 outperforms Yanshan's 6095 on your specific film line — requires evaluating dozens of sources per grade. Most traders work 5-20 supplier relationships. The full China HDPE market is much larger.
