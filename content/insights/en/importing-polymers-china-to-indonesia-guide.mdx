## Why China-Origin for Indonesian Buyers

Indonesia is Southeast Asia's largest polymer consumer by volume and one of its most import-dependent. Domestic production, dominated by Chandra Asri Petrochemical (now Chandra Asri Pacific) in Cilegon, Banten, covers only an estimated 25-30% of national PE, PP, and PVC demand. The remaining 70-75% is imported, primarily from the Middle East, Korea, Thailand, and China.

China's share of Indonesian polymer imports has grown steadily since 2022, driven by the same structural feedstock economics that are reshaping supply across ASEAN. Coal-to-olefin (CTO) producers in Ningxia and Inner Mongolia produce PE and PP at estimated cash costs $80-120/MT below naphtha-based competitors when crude oil is above $70/bbl. Coastal propane dehydrogenation (PDH) producers in Shandong, Zhejiang, and Guangdong hold a more moderate $40-70/MT advantage. For a full explanation of how these feedstock routes create structural cost differences, see our [CTO, PDH vs. Naphtha Feedstock Explainer](/insights/cto-pdh-naphtha-feedstock-advantage-resin-prices).

Three factors make China particularly relevant for Indonesian buyers in 2026:

**ACFTA eliminates import duty.** Under the ASEAN-China Free Trade Area, commodity PE, PP, and PVC from China enter Indonesia at 0% duty when accompanied by a valid Form E certificate of origin. Without Form E, MFN rates of 5-10% apply — substantially higher than Vietnam (2%) or the Philippines (3-5%), making correct documentation even more critical in Indonesia.

**Freight economics are competitive.** Shanghai to Jakarta (Tanjung Priok) is approximately 7-10 sailing days with multiple weekly direct services. This is longer than the 5-8 days to Manila or Ho Chi Minh City but considerably shorter than the 25-35 days from Middle Eastern origins via the Strait of Hormuz — a route now further complicated by the ongoing Hormuz disruption.

**Supply diversity reduces concentration risk.** Indonesian buyers who have historically relied on Middle Eastern and Korean origins face elevated risk from Hormuz-related supply disruption and Korean naphtha-cracker margin compression. China's export sector involves over 1,600 producers and thousands of trading entities, providing depth that few other origins can match. For a comparative analysis, see our [China vs. Korea vs. Middle East Origin Comparison](/insights/china-korea-middle-east-polymer-origin-comparison).

However, Indonesia is a more complex import destination than Vietnam or the Philippines for one critical reason: the SNI (Standar Nasional Indonesia) certification and Sucofindo pre-shipment inspection requirements add regulatory layers that do not exist in most other ASEAN markets. This guide walks through each component so you can calculate true landed cost and timeline before committing to an order.

## Tariff Structure: ACFTA, MFN, and HS Codes

### ACFTA Preferential Rates

Indonesia is a founding member of ACFTA, which provides zero-tariff access for most commodity polymer resins from China. The preferential rates for the most commonly traded grades:

| Product | HS Code | MFN Duty (No Preference) | ACFTA Rate (Form E) | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------------------------|----------------------|-------|
| LLDPE granules (SG &lt; 0.94) | 3901.10.xx | 5% | **0%** | Verify subheading |
| HDPE (SG >= 0.94) | 3901.20.xx | 5% | **0%** | Verify subheading |
| PP homopolymer | 3902.10.xx | 5% | **0%** | — |
| PP copolymer | 3902.30.xx | 5% | **0%** | — |
| PVC suspension | 3904.10.xx | 5-10% | **0%** | Rates vary by subheading |

Four important points about Indonesia's tariff landscape:

**MFN rates are significantly higher than Vietnam or the Philippines.** Indonesian MFN duties on commodity polymers are 5-10%, compared with 2% in Vietnam and 3-5% in the Philippines. A rejected Form E in Indonesia is expensive. On a 25-MT container of PP at $1,020/MT FOB, a 5% MFN duty on the CIF value adds approximately $1,313 to the shipment cost, or roughly $53/MT. At 500 MT per year, that amounts to approximately $26,250 in unnecessary cost. Getting Form E right is not optional in Indonesia — it is among the highest-leverage cost items a buyer controls.

**RCEP provides a backup, but with limitations.** Both China and Indonesia are RCEP signatories. If Form E encounters issues, RCEP's Form RCEP can serve as an alternative pathway to preferential treatment. However, RCEP rates for some polymer HS codes in Indonesia may not yet be at 0% — they are phasing down over the RCEP implementation schedule. Check the current RCEP rate for your specific HS code before relying on it as a backup. For a comprehensive guide to ACFTA mechanics, see our [ACFTA Tariffs, Form E & Landed Cost Guide](/insights/acfta-tariffs-form-e-landed-cost-guide).

**Chandra Asri's domestic production does not eliminate import need.** Chandra Asri produces PE (HDPE, LLDPE) and PP at its Cilegon complex, but output covers only a fraction of national demand. Domestic pricing for Chandra Asri grades often tracks import parity, meaning import prices effectively set the market floor. Buyers who source exclusively from Chandra Asri face concentration risk and limited grade availability.

**Anti-dumping investigations on PP are underway.** In December 2024, KADI (Komite Anti Dumping Indonesia) initiated an anti-dumping investigation on PP homopolymer imports (HS 3902.10.40) targeting eight countries including China, filed by PT Chandra Asri Pacific. As of March 2026, no definitive duties have been imposed — the investigation is ongoing. Separately, KADI proposed final anti-dumping duties on PP block copolymer (HS 3902.30.90) from South Korea, Vietnam, UAE, Malaysia, and Singapore at rates of 7.17-29.01%, but China is not targeted in that proceeding. Anti-dumping duties on BOPP films (HS 3920.20) from China and Malaysia were enacted in September 2024 under Circular No. 60/2024 at rates of 5.76-29.95%, but these apply to finished BOPP film products, not PP resin. Monitor KADI announcements quarterly — if the PP homopolymer investigation concludes with affirmative findings, definitive duties on Chinese-origin PP resin could materially change landed cost economics.

### HS Code Classification

Correct HS classification is critical for both duty treatment and Form E validation. The relevant Chapter 39 codes:

| Product | HS Code Range | Description |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| Polyethylene (LDPE, LLDPE) | 3901.10.xx | SG &lt; 0.94, in primary forms |
| Polyethylene (HDPE) | 3901.20.xx | SG >= 0.94, in primary forms |
| Polypropylene homopolymer | 3902.10.xx | In primary forms |
| PP copolymers | 3902.30.xx | In primary forms |
| PVC (unplasticized) | 3904.10.xx | Not mixed with other substances |

Indonesia's HS subheading extensions follow the AHTN (ASEAN Harmonized Tariff Nomenclature) structure but include Indonesia-specific 8-digit and 10-digit extensions. Before your first shipment, confirm the exact subheading with your Indonesian customs broker (PPJK — Pengusaha Pengurusan Jasa Kepabeanan) and ensure it matches across the commercial invoice, packing list, and Form E. Misclassification is the single most common trigger for customs delays.

## SNI and Sucofindo: Indonesia's Regulatory Layer

This is where Indonesia differs materially from Vietnam, the Philippines, and most other ASEAN import destinations. Two regulatory requirements add cost, timeline, and compliance complexity.

### SNI (Standar Nasional Indonesia)

SNI is Indonesia's mandatory national standard for products sold in the domestic market. For polymer resins, SNI compliance means the imported product must meet specified technical parameters (density, melt flow index, tensile strength, etc.) and carry appropriate labeling and documentation demonstrating compliance.

**Key points for polymer importers:**

- SNI requirements apply to specific polymer product categories. Not all commodity PE, PP, and PVC grades require SNI certification, but many do. The applicable SNI standards include SNI 06-0105 (for PE), SNI 06-0106 (for PP), and related standards for PVC compounds. Check the current list of SNI-mandatory products with the Badan Standardisasi Nasional (BSN) or your customs broker.
- The Chinese producer or exporter must obtain SNI certification for the specific grades being exported. This is not a one-time exercise — certification covers specific products, not the producer generally.
- SNI certification involves laboratory testing of product samples, review of production facility quality management, and issuance of a product certificate (SPPT SNI — Surat Persetujuan Penggunaan Tanda SNI).
- **Timeline:** Initial SNI certification can take 2-4 months, including sample submission, testing, and administrative processing. For established Chinese producers with existing SNI certificates, adding new grades is faster but still requires documentation.
- **Practical impact:** SNI is a barrier to entry for new suppliers but not for established trade flows. Major Chinese producers like Sinopec, PetroChina, and the large private-sector producers (Hengli, Zhejiang Petrochemical) generally maintain SNI certification for their most-traded export grades. Smaller producers and trading companies may not have current SNI coverage, which limits the grade universe available to Indonesian buyers compared to what is accessible in Vietnam or the Philippines.

### Sucofindo Pre-Shipment Inspection

Sucofindo (PT Superintending Company of Indonesia) is Indonesia's state-affiliated inspection company, authorized to conduct pre-shipment verification of conformity (PVoC) for certain imported products.

**How it works for polymer imports:**

1. The Indonesian importer applies for a Sucofindo inspection through the Indonesia National Single Window (INSW) system.
2. Sucofindo arranges inspection at the origin — either at the Chinese producer's facility or at the port of loading. Inspection covers product conformity to the declared specifications, quantity verification, and packaging/labeling compliance.
3. Sucofindo issues a Laporan Surveyor (LS — Surveyor Report) upon satisfactory inspection. The LS is required for customs clearance in Indonesia.
4. Without a valid LS, the shipment cannot clear Indonesian customs. Goods arriving without LS are subject to delays, additional inspection at destination, and potential penalties.

**Timeline and cost:**

- Pre-shipment inspection typically takes 3-7 working days from application to LS issuance, assuming the goods are ready for inspection.
- Sucofindo inspection fees are borne by the importer and typically range from $200-500 per shipment, depending on the commodity and inspection scope.
- The inspection must be coordinated with the shipment timeline. Late inspection application is the most common cause of shipment delays in the Indonesia trade.

**Practical advice:** Build Sucofindo inspection into your procurement timeline from the beginning. Notify Sucofindo (or their appointed surveyor) at least 7-10 working days before the planned loading date. Provide complete documentation including the commercial invoice, packing list, SNI certificate number, and the Chinese supplier's product specifications. A well-prepared inspection application avoids follow-up requests that extend the timeline.

## Port Logistics: Jakarta, Surabaya, and Beyond

### Primary Gateway: Tanjung Priok (Jakarta)

Tanjung Priok handles approximately 60-65% of Indonesia's containerized imports. For polymer buyers, it is the default discharge port for Java-based converters and distributors, which account for the majority of Indonesia's plastic processing industry.

**Transit times from China to Tanjung Priok:**

| Origin Port | Transit (Days) | Frequency | Service Type |
|-------------|---------------|-----------|--------------|
| Shanghai/Ningbo | 7-10 days | 5-7 sailings/week | Mostly direct |
| Shekou/Shenzhen | 5-8 days | 4-6/week | Direct |
| Guangzhou/Nansha | 5-8 days | 3-5/week | Direct |
| Qingdao | 10-14 days | 3-4/week | Direct and transshipment |

South China ports (Shekou, Nansha, Guangzhou) offer the shortest transit to Jakarta, often arriving 2-3 days faster than Shanghai/Ningbo origin. This matters for buyers managing tight production schedules or responding to spot market opportunities.

Tanjung Priok is well-served by major carriers including COSCO, Maersk, ONE, CMA CGM, Evergreen, Yang Ming, Wan Hai, and SITC. Jakarta New Priok Container Terminal (JICT) and Terminal Petikemas Koja (TPK Koja) are the primary discharge terminals.

**Congestion note:** Tanjung Priok periodically experiences congestion, particularly during pre-Ramadan and pre-Lebaran import surges. Yard utilization can exceed 85% during peak periods, adding 1-3 days of waiting time. Plan for potential demurrage during known congestion windows.

### Tanjung Perak (Surabaya)

Surabaya is the gateway for East Java's industrial base, which includes a significant concentration of plastic converters serving domestic and export markets. Transit from Shanghai/Ningbo is approximately 9-12 days, with direct services from major carriers.

For buyers in East Java (Surabaya, Gresik, Sidoarjo, Pasuruan), discharging at Tanjung Perak avoids the cost and time of trucking from Jakarta. The savings on inland transport can offset the slightly higher ocean freight rates to Surabaya.

### Belawan (Medan)

Belawan serves North Sumatra and is relevant for buyers in the Medan industrial area. Transit from China is approximately 6-9 days from South China ports, making it one of the faster routes. Service frequency is lower than Jakarta or Surabaya, and freight rates carry a modest premium.

### Freight Cost Estimates

Container freight rates from China to Indonesian ports fluctuate with market conditions. As a general planning range for 2026:

| Route | 20ft Container (USD) | Per MT (~22 MT load) |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Shanghai/Ningbo to Tanjung Priok | $200-400 | ~$9-18/MT |
| Shekou/Nansha to Tanjung Priok | $150-350 | ~$7-16/MT |
| Shanghai/Ningbo to Tanjung Perak | $250-450 | ~$11-20/MT |
| Qingdao to Tanjung Priok | $250-450 | ~$11-20/MT |

These ranges are illustrative and subject to seasonal variation, carrier allocation decisions, and fuel surcharges. South China origins consistently offer the lowest freight to Jakarta, reflecting both shorter distance and higher service frequency.

## Landed Cost: A Worked Example

To illustrate total cost, here is a step-by-step calculation for importing PP homopolymer from China to a converter's warehouse in the Cikarang industrial area (east of Jakarta) via Tanjung Priok.

**Assumptions:**
- PP homopolymer, FOB Ningbo: $1,020/MT (illustrative market-level price)
- Container: 40ft HC, approximately 25 MT load
- Ocean freight: $300/40ft HC (Q1 2026 Jakarta rate estimate)
- Insurance: 0.3% of CFR value
- ACFTA Form E: valid (0% duty)
- VAT (PPN): 11%
- Income Tax Prepayment (PPh 22 Impor): 2.5% for API holders
- USD/IDR: 15,800

| Cost Component | Per Container (USD) | Per MT (USD) | Notes |
|----------------|-------------------|-------------|-------|
| FOB Ningbo | $25,500 | $1,020 | Supplier quote |
| Ocean freight (40ft HC) | $300 | $12 | Q1 2026 range estimate |
| CFR Jakarta | $25,800 | $1,032 | FOB + freight |
| Insurance (0.3% of CFR) | $77 | $3 | Marine cargo insurance |
| **CIF Tanjung Priok** | **$25,877** | **$1,035** | Duty base |
| Customs duty (ACFTA 0%) | $0 | $0 | Form E required |
| Sucofindo inspection fee | $350 | $14 | Pre-shipment inspection |
| THC (destination) | $275 | $11 | Terminal handling at JICT |
| Documentation fees (D/O, manifest) | $150 | $6 | Carrier and agent fees |
| Customs brokerage (PPJK) | $200 | $8 | Broker fees + INSW filing |
| **Subtotal (ex-trucking, ex-taxes)** | **$26,852** | **$1,074** | |
| Trucking (Tanjung Priok to Cikarang) | $200 | $8 | ~40 km from port |
| **Total landed (ex-taxes)** | **$27,052** | **$1,082** | |
| VAT / PPN (11% on CIF + duty) | $2,846 | $114 | Creditable input VAT |
| PPh 22 Import (2.5% on CIF + duty) | $647 | $26 | Prepayment, creditable against income tax |
| **Total cash outlay** | **$30,545** | **$1,222** | Including all taxes |

**Key takeaway:** The $1,020/MT FOB price becomes approximately $1,082/MT at the warehouse gate before taxes, or approximately $1,222/MT including VAT and PPh 22 (both of which are creditable for properly registered businesses). The cost stack above CIF adds roughly $47/MT in port charges, brokerage, inspection, and trucking — slightly higher than Vietnam or the Philippines due to the Sucofindo inspection requirement.

**Excluding recoverable taxes (VAT and PPh 22), the effective landed uplift is approximately $62/MT, or 6.1% above FOB.** This is the number that matters for origin comparison.

### What Happens Without Form E

If Form E is rejected or unavailable, MFN duty of 5% applies:

| Scenario | Duty | Additional Cost per MT | Annual Impact (500 MT) |
|----------|------|----------------------|----------------------|
| With Form E (ACFTA 0%) | 0% | $0 | $0 |
| Without Form E (MFN 5%) | 5% | ~$52/MT | ~$25,875 |

The $52/MT penalty for missing or rejected Form E in Indonesia is among the highest in ASEAN, making documentation compliance a high-priority cost control item.

## Practical Recommendations

### Plan SNI Certification Early

If you are sourcing from a Chinese producer for the first time, verify their SNI certification status before placing an order. Major producers generally maintain current certificates for high-volume export grades, but coverage is not universal. If the grade you need lacks SNI certification, the application process takes 2-4 months. Factor this into your supplier evaluation timeline.

For established suppliers with existing SNI certificates, confirm the certificate is current (not expired) and covers the specific grade and HS code you intend to import. Expired or grade-mismatched SNI certificates trigger the same customs delays as missing certification.

### Coordinate Sucofindo Inspection Proactively

The most common logistical failure in China-to-Indonesia polymer trade is late Sucofindo inspection application. Build the following timeline into every order:

1. **Order confirmed** — immediately notify your Sucofindo contact or apply through INSW
2. **T-10 days before loading** — confirm inspection date with Sucofindo surveyor at origin
3. **T-5 days** — provide final documentation package (invoice, packing list, SNI certificate, COA)
4. **T-3 days** — inspection conducted at warehouse or port
5. **T-1 day** — LS (Laporan Surveyor) issued
6. **Loading day** — cargo ships with LS included in documentation set

Any delay in steps 1-3 pushes the loading date back. A shipment that misses its vessel booking due to late Sucofindo inspection costs the buyer both the re-booking fee and the opportunity cost of delayed delivery.

### Select Your Customs Broker Carefully

Indonesian customs clearance (PPJK services) involves navigating the INSW system, managing the ACFTA Form E verification process, and coordinating with both Bea Cukai (Direktorat Jenderal Bea dan Cukai — Indonesia's customs authority) and Sucofindo documentation. Not all brokers handle polymer imports with equal competence.

Prioritize brokers who: have specific experience with Chapter 39 (plastics) imports, understand the SNI verification process at Bea Cukai, can manage Form E verification including post-clearance audit preparation, and maintain established relationships at Tanjung Priok or your chosen discharge port. A broker who catches a classification or Form E error before filing saves weeks of resolution time.

### Start with a Trial Container

For first-time China sourcing, a single trial container (20ft, approximately 22 MT) is the most efficient way to validate the full logistics chain: SNI compliance, Sucofindo inspection workflow, Form E processing, Bea Cukai clearance time, and delivered grade quality. The trial container is an investment in operational data. It answers every question that desk research cannot — actual clearance time, actual total cost, and actual product quality in your specific application.

### Importer Registration: API Requirements

To import polymer resin into Indonesia, you need an active API (Angka Pengenal Importir — Importer Identification Number). There are two types:

- **API-U (Umum):** General importer license for trading companies and distributors. Allows import for resale.
- **API-P (Produsen):** Producer importer license for manufacturers/converters importing raw materials for their own production.

The PPh 22 Import tax rate differs: API-P holders pay 2.5% (as shown in the landed cost example above), while importers without API pay a higher 7.5% rate. Maintaining current API registration is both a legal requirement and a significant cost factor.

### Monitor the Regulatory Environment

Indonesia's trade policy landscape is more dynamic than some ASEAN neighbors. Key items to monitor quarterly:

- **KADI (Komite Anti Dumping Indonesia) announcements** for new investigation initiations on polymer imports
- **SNI mandatory product list updates** from BSN for changes affecting polymer HS codes
- **Sucofindo inspection scope changes** through INSW circulars
- **ACFTA tariff schedule amendments** through the Ministry of Trade (Kementerian Perdagangan)
- **PPh 22 rate adjustments** from the Ministry of Finance

A regulatory change that adds even 2-3% to landed cost can shift origin economics materially. Staying informed is part of procurement optimization.
